The Boy King of Ancient Egypt

He was not the most powerful pharaoh Egypt ever had. He did not win great battles or build towering monuments. Yet Tutankhamun is the most famous ruler in the history of Ancient Egypt — and the reason for that fame is not connected with what he achieved in life.

Tutankhamun was born around 1341 BC, the son of the controversial pharaoh Akhenaten, who had attempted to overturn Egypt's traditional religion and replace its many gods with the worship of a single deity. When Akhenaten died, the country was in disorder. Tutankhamun came to the throne at around the age of nine, making him one of the youngest rulers in Egyptian history. Advised by powerful officials, he immediately set about restoring the old religion and repairing the damage his father had caused.

His reign lasted only about ten years. Tutankhamun died at around the age of nineteen, and the cause of his death has been debated for over a century. Some researchers have pointed to evidence of a broken leg, which may have become infected. Others believe he suffered from a combination of inherited health conditions. What archaeologists do know is that his tomb is significantly smaller and simpler than those of other pharaohs of similar status, and that some of the objects buried with him appear to have been made for someone else — both signs that his burial was arranged with unusual speed.

For over three thousand years, his tomb lay undiscovered beneath the sands of the Valley of the Kings. That changed on the 4th of November 1922, when British archaeologist Howard Carter uncovered the entrance to a sealed chamber. What followed was one of the greatest archaeological discoveries in history. The tomb contained over five thousand items, including the iconic golden death mask that has since become one of the most recognised images in the world. Remarkably, unlike most royal tombs in Egypt, it had never been looted.

Today, Tutankhamun continues to fascinate scientists and historians alike. Advances in DNA analysis and medical imaging have allowed researchers to learn more about his health and family background than ever before. In 2022, a new grand museum near the pyramids of Giza was opened to house his treasures permanently. Through the remarkable preservation of his tomb, we have gained a rare and detailed glimpse into Ancient Egypt — its religious rituals, royal burial customs, craftsmanship, and the objects that surrounded those at the very top of its society. Tutankhamun never ruled long enough to leave a political legacy — but the world he left behind him has proved impossible to forget.
